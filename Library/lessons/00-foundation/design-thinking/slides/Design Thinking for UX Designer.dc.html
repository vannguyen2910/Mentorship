The real gap

The problem isn't a lack of design skill. It's a lack of design thinking.

Between knowing nothing and a confident solution sits a messy middle — understanding people, framing the real problem, exploring options. That middle is where design thinking lives.

Theory

Design Thinking is a human-centered problem-solving method — to understand needs, challenge assumptions, and create solutions.

Where process fits

Most talk lives at the bottom. Growth lives at the top.

Credit by @Artiom Dashinsky
Why process matters

Process is what makes decisions defensible

Without process

Solutions decided before the problem is understood.

  • The brief is treated as truth.
  • Assumptions stay invisible until it's too late.
  • Late pivots are expensive and demoralising.
With process

Decisions grounded in user evidence.

  • Assumptions are surfaced early.
  • Research actually changes what gets built.
  • Fewer surprises at launch.

Before next session

Set up + document

Assignment 01 · Set up your process

Build the board

  • Choose a real project to work with.
  • Add at least one activity and one output per stage.
  • Set it up in your project-management tool.
  • Share and agree on a workspace with your team.
Assignment 02 · Complete the brief

Draft the brief

  • Draft your problem statement — three versions with the AI prompt.
  • Describe your primary user in 2–3 sentences.
  • List three design goals and any known constraints.
  • Update it after your stakeholder interview.
Before next session

Capture + interview

Assignment 03 · Capture current screens

Map the flow

  • Identify key experiences — onboarding, core task, checkout, settings.
  • Screenshot each screen and label each state.
  • Arrange them in a screen-flow layout.
  • We'll use these for friction analysis in Session 2.
Assignment 04 · Stakeholder interview

Have the conversation

  • Prepare a 5–8 question script.
  • Focus on experience, pain points, constraints, success criteria.
  • Record your notes immediately after.
  • Update your design brief from what you learned.
