Homework: your stakeholder pitch
Before we start

You can't fix a system you haven't mapped.

A design decision made without a grounded picture of the current state is a guess, and guesses compound. A redesign built on unchecked assumptions is the same beliefs in a nicer interface.

01Part One

Target what you're testing

Name the assumptions that matter most before you look for evidence.

Recap

Four types. One formula.

01

User

Who they are, what they care about, how they behave.

02

Problem

The nature and severity of the problem.

03

Solution

Whether the proposed fix addresses the problem.

04

Business

Market viability, pricing, regulatory factors.

Full framework: framework-assumption-map.md
Write it right

Write assumptions you can test.

We believe [WHO] will [WHAT] because [WHY]. We'll know we're right when [SIGNAL].
Weak
“Users will find uploading easy.”
Strong
We believe first-time applicants will upload their ID on the first try because accepted formats are shown first. We'll know we're right when upload failures drop below 5%.

Write it right

Pattern, assumption, impact.

We noticed [PATTERN, with evidence] across [N sources]. This confirms or breaks our assumption that [ASSUMPTION]. It matters because [IMPACT].
Weak
“Competitors do upload differently.”
Strong
All 3 comparators show accepted formats before upload, and “upload failed” is our top ticket theme. This breaks our assumption that users know what a valid file is. It matters because upload blocks every later step.
04Part Four

Present with confidence

Lead with the headline. Every claim points at evidence.

Structure

Lead with the headline. Not the method.

Headline
3 to 4 insights, each with evidence and impact
Recommended next step
Appendix: pattern log, screenshots

One-line summary of what you researched and why. Then the insights. Then the ask.

The full log lives in the appendix. The core readout stays tight.

Each claim points at a named source. If you can't point at one, the insight isn't ready.

Defend it

“That's just your opinion.”

The answer is never to argue harder. Point at the evidence already attached to the insight. Confidence comes from preparation, not tone.

05Part Five

Practise

Four short activities on your own project. By hand, no AI.

Structure your assumptions
8

Minutes

Open your raw list from earlier sessions, or write eight fast. Rewrite five to eight with the formula. Plot each on the matrix. Share back: which one landed top-left?

Competitor scan
12

Minutes

Name your workflow and top-left assumption. Walk it in 2 to 3 comparators. Add a row per pattern: competitor, workflow, pattern, related assumption, why it matters. Group into 2 to 3 patterns with a count.

Close the loop
5

Minutes

Using your pattern worksheet and evaluation.md findings, mark each targeted assumption Confirmed, Unconfirmed or newly Unknown. Re-plot what moved. Share back which moved furthest.

Synthesise your insights
6

Minutes

Review your worksheet, evaluation.md and updated map together. Write two insight statements with the formula. Share back your strongest one and its evidence.
